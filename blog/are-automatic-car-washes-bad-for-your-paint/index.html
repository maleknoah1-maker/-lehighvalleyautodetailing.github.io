Car Care Guide

Are automatic car washes bad for your paint?

Short answer: they can be. Automatic washes are fast and cheap, but the way most of them clean is rough on paint. Here is what actually happens to your car in the tunnel, and what to do instead.

By Noah Malek, owner · October 15, 2026

Where the scratches come from

Most tunnel washes use spinning brushes or cloth strips. Those brushes touch hundreds of cars a day, and the grit from every muddy truck before you can stay in them. When they spin across your car, that grit gets dragged across the clear coat.

The result is fine, circular scratches called swirl marks. You often can't see them in the shade, but in direct sun or under a gas station light they show up as a spider web across the paint. Every trip adds a few more, and over time the paint looks dull even when it's clean.

What about touchless washes?

Touchless washes don't use brushes, so they're less likely to scratch. To clean without touching the car, though, they rely on strong chemicals and high pressure. That can strip wax and sealant faster, and it usually leaves a thin film of dirt behind that you can see once the car dries.

Other things the tunnel can do

  • Air dryers leave water spots. If water dries on the paint, the minerals stay behind as spots.
  • Trim and wheels get missed. Brushes can't reach door jambs, inner wheels or the lower panels where road grime builds up.
  • Add-ons get skipped by the brush. Spoilers, roof racks and mirrors can get caught or skipped entirely.

How to wash a car without scratching it

  • Pre-rinse or foam first. Loosen dirt before anything touches the paint.
  • Use two buckets. One with soap, one with clean water to rinse the mitt, so dirt doesn't go back on the car.
  • Use a clean microfiber mitt, not a sponge or brush, and work top to bottom.
  • Dry with a soft microfiber towel instead of letting it air dry.
  • Protect the paint. A wax or sealant makes the surface slicker, so dirt comes off easier next time.

That's the same approach we use. Our exterior packages start with a foam pre-wash, then a hand wash and hand dry, and the Full Exterior Detail adds a spray sealant that lasts about 3 to 6 months.

So should you never use an automatic wash?

If it's the difference between a clean car and a car covered in winter road salt, a wash is better than no wash. A touchless wash is the gentler choice. Just know that regular trips through a brush tunnel add up, and a proper hand wash every so often keeps the paint looking its best.

Questions

Common questions.

Do automatic car washes scratch your car?

Brush and cloth washes can. Grit trapped in the brushes gets dragged across the paint and leaves fine circular scratches called swirl marks. Touchless washes are less likely to scratch but use stronger chemicals.

Is a touchless car wash safe for paint?

It is gentler than a brush wash because nothing touches the car. The tradeoff is harsher chemicals that can strip wax and sealant faster, and it often leaves a light film of dirt behind.

How do I get rid of swirl marks?

Swirl marks are scratches in the clear coat, so washing alone won’t remove them. They are usually reduced with paint correction (machine polishing). Preventing them with safe washing habits is the easier route.

How often should I hand wash my car?

Every two weeks is a good rhythm for a daily driver, and more often in winter when road salt is on the car.
